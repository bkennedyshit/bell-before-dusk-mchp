# Bell Before Dusk — Contest Game Definition

## What the game is

**Bell Before Dusk is a portrait survival-and-resource-management micro-adventure in which a broke town courier makes two increasingly dangerous supply runs into a yokai fog, converts cedar and spirit ash into mutually exclusive ward-bell repairs, then survives a final breach using the build they created.**

Setting: a fictional Japanese post town in 1605. Tone: adventurous, supernatural, and lightly comic. The game, characters, locations, dialogue, enemies, and art are original.

## What the game is not

- It is not a miniature Zelda campaign or an open-world RPG.
- Its platform route is not a disconnected mini-game: it is the dangerous bridge to a richer overworld region.
- It does not need dialogue trees, multiple towns, farming, or a sprawling crafting inventory.
- Town is a memorable hub and tutorial space, not a second full game.

Anything that does not improve the gather-versus-danger decision, the repair decision, or the final payoff is outside the contest build.

## Target

- Primary prize lane: **Survival & Resource Management**.
- Special-award target: **Most Satisfying Progression**.
- Session length: **8–10 minutes** for a first-time judge.
- Controls: movement, katana, and contextual interaction.
- Maps: one T-shaped town, one inn, one forge, one broad haunted field used for chase and survival runs, one short Forest Spirit Path chase, and the town bell-yard finale.

## The repeated decision

The player repeatedly asks: **Do I stay in the fog to gather more, or return now with enough to survive the next escalation?**

That is the core. Katana combat protects the gathering decision; it is not a separate combat campaign. Town exploration introduces and pays off the loop; it is not an open world.

## Complete session

### 1. Town incident and escape — 45–60 seconds

- Talk to the official and receive the katana.
- A marked clapper thief runs through the main street and escapes through North Gate.
- The hero follows through that same explicit gate; no building, ladder, or arbitrary transition substitutes for it.
- The town teaches fixed-scale movement, interaction, and spatial navigation before combat pressure begins.

Moving residents, readable doors, solid roofs and walls, and dry dialogue establish the town as a safe social hub rather than a combat map.

### 2. Haunted-field pursuit — 60–90 seconds

- Chase the visible thief north through an explorable field rather than a narrow hallway.
- Yokai introduce combat while the thief follows a readable route to the upper-right torii.
- The north stone gate is visibly sealed as a demo boundary; it is not the chase destination.
- Entering the torii begins the one side-view pursuit for a physical reason: the thief has fled onto a ravine path that cannot be crossed in the top-down field.

### 3. Forest Spirit Path payoff — 60–90 seconds

- Defeat three guards while crossing grounded cedar, ravine, and shrine terrain.
- Corner the marked thief at the far torii and defeat him with three readable hits.
- Recover the clapper and return automatically to the field side of the torii.
- Walk south through the same field and explicitly use the town gate. The platform route is never used as a disguised building exit or repeated backward.

### 4. Supply run one — 90–120 seconds

- Heal or buy an upgrade in town, then re-enter North Gate explicitly.
- Gather cedar from exposed deadfall.
- Defeat readable, telegraphing yokai to collect spirit ash.
- Fog rises only in the hostile map.
- Deeper danger bands contain denser resources and stronger enemies.
- Return through the same gate once the minimum repair cost is met—or stay longer for score and safety margin.

### 5. Town services and first repair — 30–45 seconds

The town has two functional interiors:

- the Crooked Carp inn restores full health for 1 mon;
- Sparrow Forge sells a one-session whetstone for 3 mon;
- Sparrow Forge sells a one-session fog-slowing smoke charm for 2 mon;
- future version: the inn can save and resume between town visits.

The mandatory cedar-and-ash conversion occurs at the ward bell after returning.

Choose exactly one:

- **Braided Ward Rope:** fog rises 25% slower.
- **Tempered Clapper:** stronger katana and stronger bell rings.

The chosen repair becomes visible on the bell and changes play immediately.

### 6. Harder supply run — 90–120 seconds

- Re-enter the haunted field after choosing the first repair.
- Gather the larger second resource requirement under faster fog, denser nodes, and stronger yokai pressure.
- The field is spatially familiar but mechanically escalated; the story chase is not repeated.
- Return through the south town gate.
- The player feels the first repair affecting the expedition.

### 7. Second repair — 20–30 seconds

Choose exactly one:

- **Gate Talismans:** more bell integrity during the breach.
- **Warrior's Knot:** more hero health and healing when yokai are defeated.

### 8. Final breach — 90–120 seconds

- Yokai enter through North Gate.
- Wisp enemies pursue the hero; raiders prioritize the bell.
- The player must clear space and ring the bell when it is safe.
- Each ring increases resonance and visibly pushes the fog back.
- A gate yokai arrives as the final escalation.
- Defeat it and reach full resonance to win.

## Survival economy

Two resources drive the mandatory ward progression:

- **Cedar:** gathered from fixed nodes; represents physical repair material.
- **Spirit ash:** earned through combat; represents supernatural binding material.

One simple optional currency supports town services:

- **Mon:** earned from Spirit Path guards, the clapper thief, yokai, and performance bonuses; spent at the inn on healing or at the forge on katana work and smoke charms.

Cedar and ash can never be replaced by money. The player must still enter the dangerous world and perform the contest's gather-and-convert loop.

Only five survival states need constant visibility:

- hero health;
- fog pressure;
- current cedar and ash;
- current mon when near the inn or receiving it;
- bell integrity/resonance during the finale.

There is no hunger, stamina, equipment grid, durability, or sprawling crafting tree.

## Enemy roles

- **Wisp:** slow chase, obvious attack telegraph, teaches evasion and timing.
- **Prowler:** faster flanking movement, punishes standing at resource nodes.
- **Raider:** appears in the breach and attacks the bell instead of the hero.
- **Gate yokai:** final threat that combines pressure on the hero and bell.

Every enemy uses the same readable state language: notice, chase, telegraph, strike, recover, hit-stun, defeat.

## Win, lose, reset

- Win: gate yokai defeated and bell reaches 100% resonance.
- Lose: hero health reaches zero, or bell integrity reaches zero during the breach.
- Reset: outcome screen shows score, repairs chosen, survival stats, and immediate replay.

## Why it fits the judging criteria

- **Engagement:** short escalating expeditions, readable risk, responsive swordplay, and a build that pays off in one sitting.
- **Playability:** consistent top-down controls, one deliberately authored side-view route, explicit gates, solid geometry, clear objectives, and a full resettable session.
- **Core Loop:** gather, fight, decide when to return, convert resources, survive harder pressure.
- **Focus:** two mandatory repair resources, one tightly bounded shop currency, four ward upgrades, one story-motivated platform chase, and one finale. Every subsystem either teaches, funds, or changes survival odds.
- **Originality:** an Edo-period ward-bell survival run where chosen repairs alter the final defense.

## Locked scope for the contest build

- Keep exactly one required side-view Spirit Path as the climax of the opening thief pursuit; never use it as an arbitrary building exit, a shortcut around North Gate, or a repeated return route.
- Keep exactly one inn for healing/future saves and one forge for bounded equipment services.
- Do not add farming, turn-based battles, collectible monsters, additional towns, dialogue trees, or equipment inventories.
- Do not add another movement system, shop currency, or map theme until the full 8–10 minute session is fun and unbreakable.

## Definition of done

A new player can understand the objective without explanation, finish or lose within ten minutes, understand why the gate, Spirit Path, inn, and forge matter, feel both repair choices during play, identify why they were hurt, never enter roofs or walls, and immediately replay with a different build.
